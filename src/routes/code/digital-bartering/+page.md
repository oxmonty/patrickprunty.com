I come from a family who'd visit the market on weekends. There, you would barter
for what you wanted to purchase. Your intent to purchase would drive the
conversation, "How much is that blue and white poster of The Beatles album
cover?" You would ask. From there, you and the merchant would _haggle_ on a
price. Or forfeit the transaction due to being too far apart, you not willing to
put up the money for the purchase, or your intent to purchase simply not being
high enough.

The merchant, having sold the exact item or similar in the past is aware of what
a "fair price" is; given either the cost of manufacturing or what they purchased
the item for themselves. Their intent is to _sell_, and they're driven, unlike
the purchaser, not solely by intent, but a sort of soft desperation to having
sold something and made money.

The purchaser often comes away thinking they've made a bargain after
back-and-forth haggling had they driven the price down to what _they_ believed
was fair price. But often the case, the merchant still makes a profit because
and that's because they have all the past transactional data at their disposal
whereas you do not.

Bartering is a dance of sorts between the merchant and the buyer. At a high
level between the purchaser's intent and the merchant's desperation. And so why
does this dance not happen digitally?

You might argue it does happen but not on the UI surfaces the layman uses but
rather in a B2B boardroom where a deal is made for discounted price agreements
over a …

supplier. … When the layman is more accustomed to the sort of … bartering than
